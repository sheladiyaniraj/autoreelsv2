When you're generating a fresh visual for every scene in a reel, the image model you pick affects three things that matter in practice: how much each reel costs to produce, how long generation takes, and how well the image actually matches what your script is describing. Here's how Flux and Nano Banana 2 compare on all three.

## Flux

Flux (from Black Forest Labs) is a fast, general-purpose image model. It's the right default for most reel B-roll.

**Strengths:**
- Cheap: roughly half the cost per image of Nano Banana 2
- Fast generation, which matters when a reel has 6+ scenes generating in parallel
- Strong at photorealistic, straightforward scenes: a person at a desk, a city skyline, a plate of food

**Where it struggles:** prompts with multiple specific elements that all need to be correct at once, or scenes that require understanding context beyond the literal words (implied mood, unusual combinations of objects, precise spatial relationships).

## Nano Banana 2

Nano Banana 2 (Google's Gemini 3.1 Flash Image) is a multimodal model. It's built on top of a language model, not a dedicated image-only pipeline, which gives it noticeably stronger prompt comprehension.

**Strengths:**
- Better at complex or unusual scene descriptions: it "understands" the prompt more than it pattern-matches to it
- Better at maintaining consistency when a scene implies multiple related elements
- Worth the premium specifically when Flux keeps missing the brief on a particular scene

**Where it costs you:** roughly 1.5–2x the price of Flux per image, and it runs through a different generation path under the hood (multimodal chat rather than a dedicated image API), which can add a little latency.

## Head-to-head

| | Flux | Nano Banana 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per image | Lower | ~1.5–2x higher |
| Speed | Faster | Slightly slower |
| Simple, literal scenes | Excellent | Excellent |
| Complex / unusual prompts | Good | Better |
| Best default | Everyday B-roll | Scenes Flux keeps getting wrong |

## How to choose in practice

Start with Flux as your default: for the large majority of faceless-reel content (lists, tips, everyday topics), it produces perfectly good matching visuals at a lower cost. Switch to Nano Banana 2 specifically when:

- Your script has scenes with unusual or very specific combinations ("a vintage typewriter on a modern glass desk with rain visible through a window behind it")
- You've noticed Flux repeatedly producing visuals that don't quite match a particular type of scene in your content
- You're producing a smaller volume of higher-stakes content where the extra prompt accuracy is worth the added cost

There's no reason to pick one permanently. The right move is picking per reel, or even letting the choice depend on what a specific scene actually needs.
